This was my first work experience. And wasted 10.000€ of a project.

I was green as a leaf. Because of that I didn't realize all the things that make the project fail from the beginning.

SayFly! was a last mile luggage transportation company for high ticket tourists and helping people with complex luggage like disabled people.

In the tech department everything was fine, the app worked well, everything as expected and the code base
was great, the problem wasn't in the software.

This is a list of all the things that failed in SayFly! so you can take a moment and analyze them to avoid your own development.

1. [Great Software for no one.](#great-software-for-no-one)
2. [Speed > Perfection.](#speed--perfection)
3. [Knowledge over your product.](#knowledge-over-your-product)

## Great Software for no one.
Our software was perfect, it was a good piece of software no doubt there, but without clients.

The reality is that no amount of software will resist an encounter with the client, and 
the earlier you do it the better.

we didn't understand our clients, to the point we have 0 clients, the whole project was created on the idea of "build it and they will come".

But for that you have to build it for someone.

We created an empathy map, with that hypothesis you find a client so desperate that is willing to throw money at you if you fix their problem.
but by the time we created the map, it was too late and we were without time, it should have been our first priority.

## Speed > Perfection.

Another critical error was valuing perfection over iteration speed.

During my time at SayFly! (6 months) I had limited time, and being the green leaf I was, conceded to some things that I must have just said no.

**UI changes:**
We have a 4 steps form, the goal was simpler switching from 4 to 3, did this bring any value? Not yet, it may increase conversions no doubt there, but will it matter when we have 0 users? Nope, not at all.

**Discounts:**
After that, we aimed to add a discount system. Same point as before, to convert more, but again we have clients? Nope, we didn't have a clear value proposition and a clear communication of that.

Those things seem like very little and to be honest, they are, but as you will see in the next part you can kill all your speed with badly planned handoffs.

What I should have done, with our limited capability, is focus on testing faster with a real client, the earlier the better.

When I mention speed, I mean the speed you test with your client, not the actual developer skill, a 30 minutes talk with a client will teach you way more than any course.

## Knowledge over your product.

I entered SayFly! only because the original developer left, he was a freelancer and time was due.

This means that when I enter I have to acquire all the knowledge from 0. He showed me some stuff, but a large codebase was too much for me at the moment where everything was new.

So when he finally left. I was alone and that made me spend way more time fighting the framework, taking weeks in simpler bugs. This is critical for a startup.

All the advantage of a startup is speed, in your market there are bigger players for sure, but they don't have the same speed as you, when you are building something you can make a decision at the start
of the week, and by the end have it already tested.

At the enterprise level, when a senior developer leaves the org, you are not losing an experienced developer, you are losing someone that has years of experience in you, your product and your business.
When that happens at a startup level with a project with 1 to 3 years old, you are losing *all* your experience around your product.

## Conclusion

Learning is a vanity metric, but there are lessons that are better to be learned from others.
